The Challenge

Fashion has high competition, volatile demand, and lower baseline conversion rates. The objective was to scale revenue without losing control of CPC, ACoS, and traffic quality in an intensely competitive environment.

What I Did

  • Rebuilt Sponsored Products structure to separate branded, generic, and ASIN targeting for granular control
  • Scaled exact and phrase keywords that consistently converted instead of chasing volume with broad terms
  • Used search term reports weekly to add converting terms and negate wasted spend systematically
  • Controlled CPC through bid adjustments and placement tuning instead of budget cuts that sacrifice volume
  • Expanded scale gradually by increasing budgets only on campaigns hitting efficiency targets
  • Paused or reduced bids on keywords where conversion dropped as traffic increased
  • Focused spend on best-performing SKUs, avoiding dilution across low-converting ASINs

Why CPC Dropped 37%

Most brands raise CPC to scale in competitive categories. This engagement proved the opposite is possible. By focusing on high-converting search terms, improving Quality Score through better CTR and CVR, and eliminating wasteful broad traffic, CPC fell dramatically while volume increased.

Outcome

Revenue grew meaningfully in a difficult category while CPC and ACoS improved. Scaling was driven by disciplined keyword management and spend control, not aggressive bidding. This brand grew by focusing on converting search terms, efficient bids, and SKU-level discipline.

Key Takeaway

Amazon PPC growth comes from controlling what you scale, not scaling everything. This brand grew by focusing on converting search terms, efficient bids, and SKU-level discipline—proving that even in competitive categories, margin-intelligent growth is achievable.

The Challenge

Vendor Central brings unique constraints that make conventional PPC strategy ineffective:

  • No direct pricing control—Amazon retail sets prices
  • Dependence on Amazon retail buy box decisions—ads fail if buy box is lost
  • Higher CPCs driven by competitive retail auctions
  • Scaling ads without inflating TACoS across a large catalog

The goal was to grow total ordered revenue, not just ad sales, while keeping advertising efficient at Vendor scale.

Vendor-Specific Execution

  • Structured Sponsored Products and Sponsored Brands around retail-ready ASINs with stable buy box and inventory
  • Prioritized ad spend on ASINs with strong retail conversion and low return rates
  • Controlled CPC through bid discipline, avoiding aggressive bidding during retail price drops
  • Used conversion rate as the main scaling filter, not ACoS alone
  • Reduced wasted spend by actively negating low-intent and research-heavy search terms
  • Adjusted budgets based on retail performance signals rather than daily ad volatility
  • Ensured ad scale aligned with Amazon retail availability, preventing spend during constrained inventory periods

Why TACoS Stayed at 4.1%

At $3.28M in ordered revenue, most Vendor accounts see TACoS spiral as they scale advertising. This engagement kept TACoS at 4.1% by ensuring advertising supported retail momentum without overpowering it. Ad spend grew strategically, not reactively.

Outcome

The account achieved $3.28M in ordered revenue while keeping TACoS at 4.1%, proving that Vendor Central growth can be scaled responsibly. Advertising supported retail momentum without destabilizing Amazon's retail relationship or profitability.

Key Takeaway

Vendor Central success is not about pushing ads harder. It's about aligning advertising with retail readiness, conversion health, and inventory reality. This engagement delivered scale without compromising profitability or Amazon's retail partnership.
